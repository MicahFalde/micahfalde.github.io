Real photographs, and the three that need none.

You did not like the invented pictures, and you were right: they looked invented. So the top of this page is now actual photographs of rural Ohio, public domain, two of the three taken standing on Route 30. Underneath are the three you picked that need no photograph at all.

The rest follows her other notes: a picture behind the centred lockup, the name on one line (that is what made the second one chunky), and no flat navy panel. The lockup is identical in all eight. The only thing changing is what the picture is, and what it costs to get it.

Settled

Marker identity, centred lockup, name on one line, Dalton stated on arrival, three buttons.

About the pictures

These are stand-ins built in code. Each one names the real photograph it is waiting for.

What to judge

Which picture you would actually be able to take, and be happy looking at for two years.

Where that lands

Four fixes, applied to all four below. Town stated on arrival. Name on one line. A picture behind the lockup instead of a flat panel. No navy block. The marker identity and the centred layout carry over from round two, since those were already agreed.

What I would do

The recommendation

The fields. It is the only one of the four whose picture does two jobs at once: it looks like home, and it tells a stranger they are in the right county before they have read a word. Given what Kerri said about landing on churches in other states, that matters more than how pretty the sky is.

Dawn on the highway is the strongest image and the riskiest, because sunrise photos are everywhere and a mediocre one would sink it. It only works if the photograph is genuinely good.

Plaster and marker is the one to keep in your pocket. It needs no photograph at all, so it can ship the day you decide, and it can be replaced with a real picture later without rebuilding the page.

What I need from Ed

  • One of these four, or a mix.
  • Who takes the photograph, and roughly when.
  • The three button labels. Plan a visit / What we believe / Give is my guess.
  • A meeting location. Every direction currently says Dalton and nothing more.
  • Permission to photograph a service once they are meeting.
